# KA Festivals cultural guide design

## Chosen model: the shared-practice guide

Every festival detail page gains a plain-language block for someone encountering the celebration from the other country for the first time. The content is deliberately layered: a **one-sentence meaning**, up to **three common ways people may mark the day**, and a short **local-context cue**. It avoids treating a single region, family, denomination, or community as universal.

| Content layer | Coverage | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specific guide | Recurring calendar keys with a researched or high-confidence widely documented practice pattern | Use “often”, “may”, and local context notes. |
| Category guide | Every remaining record | Explain the celebration type without inventing a specific ritual. |
| Date caution | Every record | Preserve the existing verified, estimated, or review label independently from cultural explanation. |
| Visual context | Every record | Show a responsive, lazy-loaded collection visual selected by cultural/celebration group; label it as illustrative, not documentary evidence. |

## Visitor bridge

The guide does not tell a visitor how to participate in worship. Instead it explains what they may notice around the day—family visits, food, temple/gurdwara/vihara/church settings, processions, public ceremony, art, or quiet observance—and directs them to check local announcements and etiquette where appropriate.

## Visual treatment

The established Pexels collection imagery is used as a restrained story strip, not a claim that one photograph represents every observance. Detail pages add visual-group labels such as **light & lamps**, **shared table**, **offerings & quiet**, **public ceremony**, and **craft & colour**, with CSS fallbacks when a remote image cannot load. This keeps the static bundle lean and lets all records receive visual context without a large image archive.
