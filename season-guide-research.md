# Season Guide Research Notes — August 2026

## Scope and safety boundary

This guide will describe **typical broad seasonal patterns**, not a weather forecast, rainfall guarantee, agricultural recommendation, or official travel advisory. City-level variation, climate variability, and official seasonal-zone declarations remain important.

## Source-backed interface rhythm

| Country / reference area | Compact interpretation for KA Festivals | Interface boundary |
|---|---|---|
| West Bengal reference (Kolkata) | A broad eastern-India rhythm: cooler winter roughly Dec–Feb; pre-monsoon heat Mar–May; southwest monsoon Jun–Sep; post-monsoon Oct–Nov. | Present as a Kolkata/eastern-India orientation only; regional conditions vary considerably across West Bengal. |
| Jharkhand reference (Ranchi) | Cooler Nov–Feb; hot Mar to mid-Jun; a broader monsoon window from roughly Jun–Sep; post-monsoon easing into Oct–Nov. | The Jharkhand state portal explicitly describes three well-defined seasons and identifies March to mid-June as hot weather; the app uses a four-part interface rhythm to make the seasonal transition legible. [2] |
| Jakarta | Typical local rainy rhythm Nov–Mar, transition around Apr, drier rhythm May–Sep, transition around Oct. | Use “typical” language only and point visitors to BMKG for current official conditions. |
| Palangka Raya | Broad rainy rhythm Oct–May and comparatively drier Jun–Sep. | Explicitly note that local rain/storms can still occur during a drier period. |
| Banjarmasin | Broad rainy rhythm Nov–Apr, drier May–Aug, transition Sep–Oct. | BMKG publishes seasonal timing by local *Zona Musim*; do not claim a single fixed onset date. |

## Design implications

The new route should show an India/Indonesia toggle, local place tabs, a January–December band, and a one-sentence casual explanation per place. Its visual role is a bright paper “season fieldbook,” intentionally lighter than the Solar Ledger. A compact field-image strip may use carefully selected material cues—waterline, sal tree, tropical rain, dry grass, or cloud—only as labelled illustrative background, never as proof of present local conditions.
