# Research-led enhancement: layered seasonal orientation

## What the research supports

The reviewed guidance treats cultural information as interpretation rather than a flat catalogue. It recommends a clear overarching idea, a small number of key messages, multiple entry depths, and an experience that lets people explore at their own pace. It also stresses that heritage interpretation should make room for different perspectives, use plain language, and layer key information before deeper detail.[1]

For a cultural calendar, this supports a **season lens** between the daily answer and the larger archive: visitors should be able to see what the selected month holds, why its records matter, and then continue into a filtered monthly index. This creates a calm narrative progression of *today → this month → individual note* rather than asking visitors to confront a long ledger first. Art UK's digital storytelling guidance similarly favours human context, short readable sections, obvious headings, and curiosity-led internal journeys over isolated metadata.[2]

The date control will remain an editable native input with an explicit date-format hint and visible label. That follows date-picker guidance that supports direct typing, clear format explanation, accessible customisation, and no disruptive auto-submission.[3] The interface will continue to pair labels and status text with colour rather than relying on hue alone, and preserve logical interactive order and focus treatments.[4]

## Design response

The next extension adds a bilingual **Season Lens** to the daily companion. It derives a small month-specific set of records from the existing sourced calendar data and adds one clear path to a matching monthly chapter in the cultural index. The index will understand `year`, `month`, and `country` query parameters, show the selection as active context, and offer one simple return to all months. This increases orientation and storytelling without inventing cultural facts, duplicating content, or adding a dense new dashboard.
